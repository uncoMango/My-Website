# WEBSITE GROWTH STRATEGY

This document explains how people discover the ministry, receive help, return, build trust, and eventually support or share the ministry.

It is not a technical document, and it is not an aggressive sales plan. It follows and must remain consistent with two governing documents: `docs/WEBSITE_VISITOR_BLUEPRINT.md` (how visitors should experience the site) and `docs/WEBSITE_CONTENT_GUIDE.md` (how the ministry writes and speaks). Nothing in this document should ever contradict either of those.

---

## Purpose

Growth means helping more people — not merely increasing traffic, subscribers, or sales. A month where fewer people visited but more of them were genuinely helped is a better month than a month where traffic spiked but nobody's life was actually touched.

The guiding principle:

> *"Help the people and make the money. Never use the people to make money."*

Growth must remain pono, truthful, useful, and ministry-centered at every step. If a growth idea only works by treating people as a means to an end, it is not a growth idea this ministry can use — no matter how effective it might be.

Do not treat anything in this section as more finished than it actually is. When in doubt, check the Technical Blueprint before repeating a claim about what "exists."

## First Visit

A first-time visitor's experience is already documented in full in `docs/WEBSITE_VISITOR_BLUEPRINT.md` and should not be duplicated here. In short: a visitor should quickly understand who Kahu Phil is, why the ministry exists, what help is available, and where to begin.

The homepage now offers four pathways for a visitor to choose where to begin, matching the ministry's four primary paths:

- **Kingdom**
- **Health**
- **Stewardship**
- **Free Resources**

Growth strategy should always point new visitors toward one of these four paths, never around them.

## Trust

Trust is built the same way described in the Visitor Blueprint and Content Guide — through helpful teaching, real stories, honesty, Scripture, consistency, Hawaiian authenticity, no hype, and no manipulation, along with resources that are given generously and freely.

No growth tactic should ever be allowed to erode any of these. If a tactic would grow numbers while quietly damaging trust, it is not worth doing.

---

## Return Visits

People have real reasons to come back to this ministry:

- Continuing teaching series (such as the Kingdom Series).
- Rotten Fencepost content, as it develops.
- New Scripture studies and articles.
- New free resources as they're released.
- Email updates that genuinely serve rather than just remind people the ministry exists.
- Practical guidance they can actually use.

No publishing schedule (weekly articles, monthly newsletters, etc.) is documented or verified anywhere in this repository. Until one is actually established and confirmed, none should be assumed or promised to visitors.

---

## Email Relationship

Email exists to serve, not to sell. Its real purposes are to teach, to notify people of genuinely new and useful things, to invite people back to the site, and — occasionally, and only when it's honestly relevant — to introduce a helpful product or partnership opportunity.

What this looks like in practice, based on what's actually built: a footer signup form and a dedicated free-guide signup collect subscribers; a welcome email and two follow-up emails (roughly three and seven days later) can go out automatically. Beyond that first short sequence, there is currently no built system for ongoing, regular email content.

Avoid constant selling, artificial urgency, and excessive frequency. An email that exists only to remind someone the ministry is still there, with nothing new to offer, is not worth sending.

**Future Work:** a durable, ongoing email nurture sequence beyond the first welcome/follow-up messages does not currently exist and should be labeled as Future Work until it is actually built. (The Technical Blueprint also notes that the current day-3/day-7 follow-up emails rely on an in-process timer that does not survive a server restart — a real, current limitation worth knowing before any larger email strategy is planned around it.)

## Natural Support Path

The appropriate progression, and the only one this ministry should build toward:

1. A person receives real help — a free resource, a piece of teaching, an honest answer to something they were carrying.
2. Trust develops, naturally, over time.
3. The person explores more teaching, on their own initiative.
4. A useful paid resource may be offered, only once it's relevant to what they're already interested in.
5. The person may choose to partner, purchase, share, or simply keep learning for free, indefinitely, with no penalty for never giving anything back.

Support — financial or otherwise — must always be optional and pressure-free. A visitor should never feel that step 5 was the real point of steps 1 through 4.

## Measurement

Healthy growth should be measured by more than raw traffic. Practical measures worth tracking:

- Real visitors (not just page views).
- Returning visitors.
- Resource downloads (free booklets, guides).
- Email signups.
- Video views, where video is embedded.
- Completed purchases.
- Partner support.
- Meaningful engagement — time spent, depth of reading, whether someone moves from a free resource into deeper teaching.

As documented in the Technical Blueprint, Google Analytics is currently installed sitewide, so basic traffic and page-level data is available. No dashboard, report, or regular review process for this data was found in the repository — any specific measurement routine should be documented separately once one actually exists.

## What Growth Must Never Become

- Manipulation.
- Fake scarcity.
- Fear-based selling.
- Pressure of any kind.
- Misleading claims.
- Chasing trends that contradict the mission.
- Using people only as a source of revenue.

If a growth idea requires any of these to work, it does not belong here — regardless of how well it might perform.

---

## Review Checklist

Before approving any growth idea, ask:

- [ ] Does it help people?
- [ ] Is it truthful?
- [ ] Is it pono?
- [ ] Does it fit the Visitor Blueprint?
- [ ] Does it fit the Content Guide?
- [ ] Can the ministry realistically maintain it?
- [ ] Does it create a clear next step for the visitor?
- [ ] Does it preserve trust?

If any answer is no, the idea isn't ready — no matter how promising it looks.

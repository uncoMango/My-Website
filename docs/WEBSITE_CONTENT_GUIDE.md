# WEBSITE CONTENT GUIDE

> *"Help the people and let the finances follow. Never use the people to make the money."*

This is the editorial standard for every page, article, product, email, blog post, YouTube description, and future piece of content published under Ke Aupuni O Ke Akua.

It is not a technical document, and it is not a marketing guide. It exists for one reason: to preserve the ministry's voice, so that a reader hears the same ministry no matter which page they land on — whether the words were written by Kahu Phil, by an editor, or drafted with the help of a tool like ChatGPT or Claude.

---

## Purpose

This document preserves the voice, philosophy, and writing standards of Ke Aupuni O Ke Akua.

Its purpose is consistency. A reader who reads the homepage, then a free booklet, then a product page, then an email, should never feel like they've switched ministries partway through. The words may come from different pages, different formats, even different writers — but they should always sound like they came from the same place, with the same heart behind them.

---

## The Heart of the Ministry

Every piece of content, before it is anything else, should reflect these:

- **Help people first.** Before anything is sold, taught, or asked for, the first question is whether this actually helps the person reading it.
- **Let the finances follow.** Provision is the result of faithful ministry, not the goal writing is aimed at.
- **Everything must remain pono.** Right order, right conduct, right relationship — with God, with the reader, and with the truth itself.
- **Present the Kingdom of God before promoting products.** The teaching comes first. What's for sale comes after, and only in service of the teaching.
- **Teach before asking.** A reader should already have received something of real value before they're ever asked for anything.
- **Build trust before inviting support.** Trust is earned through honest, useful content over time — it is never assumed or demanded.
- **Truth is more important than popularity.** If the true thing and the popular thing are ever in conflict, truth wins, every time.
- **Never exaggerate.** Say exactly what is true, not what sounds more impressive.
- **Never manipulate.** A reader should always be free to think for themselves and walk away unpersuaded, without the content having tried to trap them into agreement.

---

## Writing Voice

The tone across everything should be:

Warm. Pastoral. Honest. Respectful. Practical. Hopeful. Easy to understand.

Never arrogant. Never condescending. Never academic simply to sound intelligent.

Write as if speaking to one person sitting across the table from you — not to a crowd, not to an audience, not to a market. One real person, listening.

---

## Reading Level

Aim for roughly a 5th–8th grade reading level.

Prefer short sentences over long, complicated ones. Say the true thing in the plainest way it can be said.

Explain difficult ideas using ordinary, everyday language — not by dumbing them down, but by translating them so a reader without any special background can still follow every step.

Avoid unnecessary theological jargon. If a technical Biblical word genuinely needs to be used, explain it plainly the moment it appears — never assume the reader already knows it.

---

## Scripture

Scripture is central to everything this ministry writes. It is never used merely as decoration — never dropped in as a proof-text to sound credible and then left unexplained.

When Scripture is used, take the time to:

- **Explain context** — what was actually happening when these words were written or spoken.
- **Explain meaning** — what the passage is actually saying, in plain language.
- **Explain application** — what difference this makes for the reader's actual life, today.

Do not assume the reader already understands Biblical terms, references, or background. Explain what needs explaining, every time.

---

## Original Languages

Hebrew, Greek, and Aramaic are valuable tools for going deeper into what Scripture actually says — not a way to sound more credentialed.

Use original-language words only when they genuinely improve the reader's understanding of a passage. When they are used, always explain them immediately in plain English. Never drop an untranslated Hebrew or Greek word into a sentence just to impress the reader — if it isn't explained, it shouldn't be there.

---

## Hawaiian Language

Use Hawaiian words naturally, the way they naturally belong in Kahu Phil's own voice and life — never as decoration or as a stylistic flourish borrowed for effect.

Always preserve proper spelling, including the ʻokina (ʻ) and kahakō (macron) marks. These are not optional styling — they are part of the correct spelling of the word.

Whenever a Hawaiian term appears and a reader unfamiliar with the language might not know it, explain what it means, briefly and respectfully, right where it appears.

Honor Hawaiian culture with real respect. Never write from a tourist's perspective — Hawaiian language and values belong in this ministry because they are part of who Kahu Phil is, not because they make the content feel more "island."

---

## Storytelling

Personal stories are encouraged and valuable. Ranching, paniolo life, ministry on Molokaʻi, family, and everyday experience are all good material for illustrating Biblical truth.

Stories should teach. A story earns its place in a piece of content by making a truth clearer or more real for the reader — not by being interesting on its own.

Stories should never glorify the storyteller. If a story makes Kahu Phil look impressive but doesn't actually help the reader understand something true, it doesn't belong.

---

## Products

Products exist to serve people. Books, studies, planners, software, and other resources support the ministry — they are never the primary purpose of it.

Whenever content touches on a product:

- Teach first.
- Help first.
- Offer the resource naturally, as the next honest step for someone who wants to go deeper — not as the point of the page.
- Avoid aggressive sales language entirely.

---

## Calls to Action

A call to action should feel like an invitation, never a demand.

**Use language like:**
- Learn more
- Continue reading
- Download a free booklet
- Watch the next lesson
- Explore this study
- Partner with the ministry

**Avoid:**
- Buy now
- Limited time
- Don't miss out
- Act immediately
- Any language built around pressure

If a call to action would make the reader feel rushed or cornered, rewrite it.

---

## Visual Style

The writing that accompanies images, videos, and page layouts should be:

Simple. Authentic. Clean. Readable.

Avoid clutter — too many competing messages on one screen. Avoid unnecessary animation or motion that exists only to grab attention. Avoid distracting wording that exists to catch the eye rather than to communicate something true.

---

## What We Never Do

- Never manipulate.
- Never shame.
- Never use fear to sell.
- Never exaggerate results.
- Never create fake urgency.
- Never use clickbait headlines.
- Never sacrifice truth for popularity.

---

## Editorial Checklist

Before publishing anything, ask:

- [ ] Is it true?
- [ ] Is it helpful?
- [ ] Is it understandable?
- [ ] Is it Biblical?
- [ ] Is it pono?
- [ ] Does it sound like Kahu Phil?
- [ ] Does it help someone?
- [ ] Would I be comfortable saying these words to someone's face?

If the answer to any of these is no, the content isn't ready yet — no matter how good it otherwise looks.

# WEBSITE VISITOR BLUEPRINT

This document is not a technical blueprint. It is not a coding document. It is not an implementation guide.

It exists to explain how a visitor should experience the Ke Aupuni O Ke Akua website — and why.

This document is meant to guide future content decisions, page organization, navigation, and visitor experience. It should make sense to Kahu Phil, to future developers, to content writers, and to ministry leaders alike. Anyone reading this should come away understanding the same thing: what this website is for, and who it is for.

---

## Purpose

This document describes the ideal visitor experience for Ke Aupuni O Ke Akua.

The website exists to serve people. Technology exists only to support that purpose — nothing more. A website, a button, a page, a form — none of it matters on its own. It only matters if it helps a real person find what they came looking for.

Every future decision about this website should be tested against one question:

**"Does this make it easier for someone to receive help?"**

If a change makes the site prettier, faster, or more modern but does not make it easier for a real visitor to be helped, it is not worth making. If a change looks small or unglamorous but removes confusion or builds trust, it is worth making.

---

## The Mission

These principles are not marketing language. They are the standard this ministry is held to.

- **Help people first.** Every page, every resource, every word should be useful to the person reading it — whether or not they ever give anything back.
- **Let the finances follow.** The ministry is resourced as a result of genuine help given, not the other way around. Provision follows faithfulness; it is not manufactured through pressure.
- **Everything must remain pono.** Right relationship, right conduct, right order. If something would need to be hidden or spun to look acceptable, it does not belong on this site.
- **Never manipulate visitors.** No dishonest framing, no guilt, no exploiting fear or urgency to move someone toward a decision.
- **Never pressure visitors.** A visitor should always feel free to leave, to say no, to come back later, or to never come back — without the site making that feel wrong.
- **Build trust before asking for support.** Support — financial or otherwise — is only ever asked for after real value has already been given, never before.
- **Teach before selling.** If a page sells something, it should have already taught something first.
- **Make Biblical truth understandable.** Depth is not the enemy of clarity. Thirty years of study in the original languages exists to make Scripture *more* accessible to ordinary people, not less.
- **Honor Hawaiian culture without becoming tourist-oriented.** Hawaiian language, values, and identity belong in this ministry because they are Kahu Phil's own — not as decoration, and never packaged for a tourist's idea of "island life."

## Who We Serve

The site serves several different kinds of people, and most visitors will belong to more than one of these groups at once. No single page needs to serve everyone — but the site as a whole needs to have a clear, honest home for each of them.

**People searching for God.** They may not have religious language for what they're feeling. They are not looking for a church program. They are looking for something real.

**Christians wanting deeper Kingdom teaching.** They already believe. They are tired of shallow teaching and want to go deeper — into Scripture, into the original languages, into what Jesus actually said about the Kingdom.

**People seeking better health.** They have likely tried diets and programs that didn't last. They are looking for something rooted in more than willpower — something that makes sense of *why* those approaches failed.

**People wanting to become better stewards.** Of money, of time, of what has been placed in their hands. They are tired of being told to simply tithe more, and want to understand the deeper Kingdom framework behind stewardship.

**Pastors and ministry leaders.** They are looking for tools, teaching, and resources that will help them serve their own congregations and communities better.

**Hawaiians wanting culturally respectful resources.** They want teaching and tools that treat Hawaiian language, values, and identity with the seriousness and respect they deserve — not as a theme.

**People discovering Rotten Fencepost.** They arrived through a specific message or story, not necessarily through the ministry's core teaching. They deserve the same honesty and the same open door to everything else here.

**People interested in Ke Kākau.** They are drawn to the deeper cultural and spiritual meaning carried in this word and the tradition behind it, and are looking for a resource that treats it with real understanding rather than surface interest.

A visitor rarely fits neatly into just one of these boxes. Someone searching for God may also be struggling with their health. A pastor may also be a Hawaiian looking for culturally grounded teaching. The site should never force a visitor to pick a single identity before it will help them — it should let them find their own way in, from wherever they are standing.

## First Impression

In the first few seconds on this website, a visitor should feel:

**Safe.** Nothing here is trying to trick them or trap them.

**Welcome.** This is a place for them, whoever they are and whatever brought them here.

**Trusted.** They are treated as capable of making their own decisions, in their own time.

**Not pressured.** No one is being pushed toward a purchase, a signup, or a decision before they are ready.

**Not overwhelmed.** The page in front of them does not demand that they take in everything the ministry has ever produced all at once.

**Not confused.** They are not left wondering what kind of website this even is.

Within those first few seconds, a first-time visitor should be able to answer three simple questions:

1. **Who is Kahu Phil?**
2. **Why does this ministry exist?**
3. **Where do I begin?**

If a visitor cannot answer those three things quickly, the homepage has not done its job — no matter how much valuable content sits below it.

## The Visitor Journey

Every page on this website — not just the homepage — should answer three questions for the person reading it:

- **Why am I here?** (What is this page, and does it match what I was looking for?)
- **What problem does this page solve?** (What will I understand, gain, or be able to do after reading it?)
- **Where should I go next?** (What is the next honest, helpful step for me?)

No visitor should ever reach a dead end — a page that answers nothing, offers nowhere else to go, and leaves them to figure out the rest of the site on their own. Every page should open a door to the next step of someone's journey, even if that next step is simply "go deeper into this same subject."

---

## The Four Primary Paths

Nearly everything this ministry teaches or offers connects into one of four primary paths. These are not marketing categories — they reflect how the ministry itself is actually structured.

**Kingdom** — What Jesus actually taught about the Kingdom of God, drawn from thirty years of study in the original Hebrew and Greek. This is the theological foundation everything else stands on.

**Health** — Kingdom wellness: the body as something to be stewarded, not controlled. Rooted in Scripture and in lived experience on Molokaʻi.

**Stewardship** — Kingdom principles for money, time, and resources. Not tithing as an obligation, but stewardship as a way of life.

**Free Resources** — The booklets, teaching, and tools offered at no cost, with no obligation, as an on-ramp into everything else.

Every new page, teaching, or resource that gets added to this site in the future should be able to say clearly which of these four paths it belongs to — or how it connects two or more of them together. If something cannot be connected to at least one of these four paths, it may not belong on this website, or it may be pointing toward a fifth path that deserves its own honest recognition rather than being forced to fit.

## The Front Door

The homepage has completed its first phase of renovation — **Homepage Phase One.**

What changed:

- **A welcome was added** near the very top of the page, so a first-time visitor is greeted before they are asked to explore anything.
- **A short personal introduction** from Kahu Phil now appears immediately — who he is, and why this ministry exists — in his own voice.
- **Four visitor pathways were added**, inviting the visitor to choose where to begin based on what actually brought them to the site: understanding the Kingdom, strengthening their health, practicing stewardship, or starting with something free.
- **All existing content was preserved.** Nothing was deleted — the announcements, the ecosystem explanation, the longer personal story, the three pillars, and the free resources are all still there, simply placed in a more visitor-friendly order.
- **First-time visitor confusion was reduced.** Previously, a new visitor encountered several offers and announcements before ever being welcomed or told where to begin. Now, the welcome and the four pathways come first.

**Why these changes were made:** a first-time visitor was being asked to make sense of the whole ministry before anyone had said hello. The homepage's job is not to display everything the ministry has ever made — its job is to greet the person who just arrived, help them understand where they are, and point them toward the one next step that matches why they came. Everything the site already offered is still there. It simply now comes *after* the welcome, not before it.

This is Phase One of an ongoing renovation. Future phases should be recorded in the Change History section below as they are completed.

## Trust Builders

These are the things that build real trust with a visitor — not tactics, but the natural result of doing ministry honestly.

- **Authenticity** — this is one pastor's real voice, real study, and real conviction, not a brand.
- **Honesty** — no exaggeration, no half-truths, no claims that outrun what is actually true.
- **Real ministry** — a real congregation, a real community on Molokaʻi, real pastoral work happening whether or not anyone online ever sees it.
- **Real stories** — Kahu Phil's own story, told plainly, without needing to be dressed up.
- **Scripture** — teaching that is anchored in the text itself, not just opinion wearing a Bible verse.
- **Helpful teaching** — content that leaves a visitor better equipped than before they arrived, whether or not they ever buy anything.
- **Clear navigation** — a visitor who can find what they need without frustration trusts the site more, not less.
- **No hype** — confidence in the message itself, without needing to inflate it.

## What We Avoid

These are intentional boundaries, not accidents of an unfinished website. They should hold even when they would be easier — or more profitable — to cross.

- Manipulation, in any form.
- Fear-based marketing.
- False urgency.
- Countdown timers.
- Clickbait.
- Tourist stereotypes of Hawaiian culture.
- Pressure sales.
- Confusing navigation.
- Information overload.

If a future idea for the site requires any of these to work, it is not the right idea for this ministry — even if it would perform well by ordinary marketing standards.

---

## Content Philosophy

**Teach first.** Every piece of content should stand on its own as something genuinely worth someone's time, whether or not it leads to anything else.

**Help first.** The test of a page is not "did it convert," but "did it help."

**Give generously.** Free resources are not bait — they are an honest gift, offered because the ministry believes what God has given it belongs to more than a sales funnel.

**Products support the ministry.** Books, planners, and other resources exist to help fund the work and to serve the people who want them.

**Products never become the ministry.** The moment a product starts shaping what gets taught, rather than the teaching shaping what gets offered, something has gone wrong. The teaching leads. The products follow.
